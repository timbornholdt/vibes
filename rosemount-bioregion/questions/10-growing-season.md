# How long is the growing season where you live?

## Short answer
Plan on a frost-free growing season of **roughly 160-170 days (about 5 months)**, running from the end of April / early May to early-to-mid October. Climate scientists at the Minnesota DNR cite the Twin Cities airport (MSP) long-term average as about **172 days**; the older 1981-2010 average was 167. Rosemount sits on cool, open outwash sand away from the downtown heat island, so a typical year there is probably somewhat shorter than the airport (my estimate: 150-165 days). Hardiness zone is **5a** on the 2023 USDA map for the northern metro, but Rosemount may sit on the 4b/5a boundary (verify, see below).

## The details

### Key numbers
| Measure | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Average last spring freeze (32°F), MSP | ~April 24 | FOX 9 / NWS-based figure for the Twin Cities. Older sources give ~May 1. |
| Average last spring frost, Rosemount/Apple Valley | ~May 3 | Old Farmer's Almanac, 1981-2010 NOAA data, "30% chance of frost after" this date. |
| Average first fall freeze (32°F), MSP | ~October 11 | MPR; about 90% of years have hit 32°F by Oct 28. |
| Growing season (freeze-free), MSP long-term average | ~172 days | MPR 2025 (1981-2010 average was 167). |
| 2025 actual at MSP | 199 days | Last freeze April 8 (24°F), first freeze October 24 (30°F). 4th longest on record. |
| Record longest at MSP | 219 days (2016) | First freeze Nov 18, 2016, a record by 11 days. |
| 2026 fall so far | no 32°F at MSP as of Oct 26 | Another very late fall; check the final date (see below). |
| USDA Hardiness zone (2023 map) | 5a (-20 to -15°F average annual extreme minimum) | Previous 2012 zone 4b (-25 to -20°F). Northern Dakota County was moved to 5a; Rosemount's exact cell needs checking. |

### 32°F versus 28°F
"Frost" and "freeze" thresholds differ by plant:
- **32°F (light freeze)** kills tender plants (tomatoes, basil, squash, beans). This defines the "growing season" figures above.
- **28°F (hard freeze)** ends the season for most hardier garden crops and ornamentals. In the Twin Cities the average 28°F date comes about 1-2 weeks after the first 32°F in fall and 1-2 weeks before the last 32°F in spring, so cool-season crops (kale, spinach, peas) can be grown far beyond the 32°F season. I did not find a published 28°F average for a Rosemount-specific station; the DNR tables below list it.
- **Frost-free days** (28 vs 32 vs 24°F) differ by up to about three weeks depending on which threshold you pick. Always check which definition a source uses.

### Why Rosemount may differ from the airport
Cold air pools on clear, calm nights over sandy, well-drained, flat ground with little urban heat, so rural Dakota County sites usually record later last-spring and earlier first-fall freezes than MSP. The Almanac's Rosemount value (May 3) is about a week later than the MSP figure from the same family of data. Local low spots, river valleys (Vermillion), and a sheltering house wall all shift dates by several days.

### Growing degree days (GDD)
GDD measure accumulated heat for crops and insects. The standard "base 50°F" formula is the daily average of the max (capped at 86°F) and min (floored at 50°F), minus 50. Normal May-September base-50 GDD for the Twin Cities are on the order of 2,500-2,800 (typical for SE Minnesota); I could not retrieve a Rosemount-specific number, so treat this as a rough range and use the DNR GDD tool or the UMN St. Paul/Rosemount station for exact values. Corn needs about 2,200-2,700 GDD to reach maturity (hybrid dependent), which fits the area's reputation as a solid corn-and-soy region.

### Trends
The freeze-free season in Minnesota has lengthened measurably (DNR notes about 16 days from 1951 to 2012, mostly from earlier springs). The 2016 season (219 days), 2025 (199 days) and 2026 (no freeze as of Oct 26) show how variable and extreme recent years have been.

## How to find and verify this yourself
1. **DNR freeze-date tables (best, official, 1991-2020 normals).** Go to the Minnesota DNR State Climatology Office, Climate > "Summaries and publications" > "Last spring / first fall freeze/frost dates" (`dnr.state.mn.us/climate/summaries_and_publications/freeze_date.html`). Click the map marker nearest Rosemount (try Hastings, Farmington, or "Twin Cities"/St. Paul stations). Allow pop-ups; a PDF opens. In the **spring** table read the "32" row and the 0.50 column for the median last freeze; read "28" for the hard freeze. In the **fall** table do the same.
2. **NWS Twin Cities / NOWData.** weather.gov/mpx > Climate > NOWData > "Freeze dates" (the "Threshold" option) or the "First and last freeze" product for Minneapolis Area. Note that the data span 1872 onward at MSP.
3. **Hardiness zone.** Go to planthardiness.ars.usda.gov, enter ZIP 55068, and read the zone (5a vs 4b) and the temperature range. For parcel-level detail, use the interactive map and zoom to your home; zone lines pass through Dakota County and zip-level answers can be misleading.
4. **Observe it yourself.** Keep a one-line garden log: first date a min/max thermometer (placed ~5 ft up, in a shaded spot) reads 32°F in fall and last in spring. Notice the date lilacs leaf out (early-mid April) and when the maple/oak leaves drop. Your own yard may differ from the airport by 3-10 days.
5. **UMN Extension.** The "Growing season" and "Frost dates" pages from UMN Extension (extension.umn.edu) explain planting windows and confirm local dates for your county.
6. **Caveats.** Almanac-style websites often use a 30% probability, 1981-2010 data, and the nearest big station, so numbers can be a week off. Always note the threshold (32/28/24°F), the probability level (50%, 30%, 10%), and the normal period.


## Uncertainties and caveats
- No published Rosemount-station freeze statistics were retrieved. The Rosemount values here rest on the Almanac (May 3, 1981-2010) and the MSP airport records, and my "150-165 day" local estimate is an informed guess, not a measurement.
- MSP average last-freeze dates differ by source (Apr 24 vs ~May 1) depending on the period (1991-2020 vs earlier) and on how "average" is defined (median vs mean).
- The 28°F averages and the GDD normals were not retrieved from primary sources; the ranges stated are approximate.
- Hardiness zone: sources agree the northern Dakota County area is 5a, and Rosemount is on the southern edge of that area. Confirm with the USDA ZIP lookup. Note that zones measure winter cold, not season length.
- 2026's first fall freeze had not happened at MSP as of Oct 26, 2026, and I did not confirm whether it has occurred since.
