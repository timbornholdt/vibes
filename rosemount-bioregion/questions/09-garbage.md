# Where does your garbage go?

## Short answer
Your hauler is Highland Sanitation. I was not able to find any public document stating which landfill, transfer station, or recycling sorting plant Highland uses for Rosemount customers, so the exact destination is **unconfirmed**. What can be said with confidence: Dakota County has two big landfills that accept ordinary household trash (Pine Bend, Inver Grove Heights, owned by Republic Services; and Burnsville Sanitary Landfill, owned by Waste Management), and nearly all curbside trash from the county that is not burned goes to metro landfills like these. One phone call to Highland settles it.

## The details

### What is and is not confirmed
- **Confirmed:** Dakota County's 2024 tonnage. About 518,000 tons of solid waste were managed in 2024: roughly 130,000 tons recycled, 155,000 tons of organics (food scraps and yard waste), 12,000 tons "resource recovery", and 221,000 tons landfilled. By my arithmetic that is about 43% landfilled and about 55% recycled plus composted. (The report itself gives tons, not percentages.)
- **Confirmed:** Dakota County has landfills of several types. A Star Tribune overview lists Pine Bend (Republic Services, Inver Grove Heights, regular garbage), Burnsville Sanitary Landfill (Waste Management, regular garbage), the Rosemount landfill (SKB Environmental; industrial, demolition and construction waste, not household trash), a mothballed SKB site, and Frattalone's demolition landfill. Note this article is several years old; ownership and status can change.
- **Not confirmed:** Highland Sanitation's disposal site, its recycling sorting facility, and which organics processor it uses. The county's progress report names no disposal or sorting facilities, and Waste Management's Rosemount page names none either.
- **Not confirmed:** Whether Rosemount's residential service is open-market (you pick a hauler) or organized by the city. Check with the city.

### Why the likely destination is a Dakota County landfill
Metro garbage mostly goes one of three ways: waste-to-energy burners (Hennepin County's plant in Minneapolis, Ramsey/Washington's plant in Newport, and, until it closed, Elk River), landfills, or recycling/composting. Counties with burners direct trash there; Dakota County has none and relies on landfills. Metro landfills in the area are Pine Bend and Burnsville plus a few others around the metro (Elk River, Dem-Con in Shakopee area). Haulers often take trash first to a transfer station, then long-haul it to a landfill. Highland Sanitation is listed in business directories as based in Newport, so it may use a nearby transfer site or a facility of the Ramsey/Washington burner system. That is speculation: treat it as a hypothesis to test.

### The landfill picture is tight
Regulators have repeatedly warned that metro landfill space is running short. The MPCA recommended expansions of the four south-metro landfills (Pine Bend, Burnsville, Dem-Con, Rich Valley) totaling about 5.6 million tons over seven years, and one article noted Burnsville had only 18 to 20 months of space left at the time. When the Elk River burner's closure was feared, regulators estimated landfills would be near permitted capacity within about six years. Expect the answer to "where does it go" to shift as sites fill, expand, or close.

### Recycling and organics
- Recycling goes to a materials recovery facility (MRF) where it is sorted; I could not identify which MRF Highland uses.
- Dakota County residential food scraps from its 11 drop-off sites go to an unnamed commercial compost facility. Curbside organics service depends on your hauler and city.
- The Recycling Zone in Eagan takes household hazardous waste and hard-to-recycle items. The county plans a "Recycling Zone Plus" in Lakeville (with Scott County), targeting 2027.

### The policy frame
Minnesota's waste hierarchy ranks reduction, reuse, recycling, and composting above burning and landfilling. State law sets a 75% recycling goal for metro counties by 2030 (recycling plus organics). Dakota County's 2018-2038 Solid Waste Master Plan sets its programs, and a 2024-2044 plan has been submitted to the MPCA.

## How to find and verify this yourself
1. **Ask the hauler (best method).** Call Highland Sanitation & Recycling (look up the current number on your bill) and ask: "Which facility receives my trash, my recycling, and my organics (if any)?" Haulers know this; ask for the facility name and city. Ask a follow-up: "Is trash taken to a transfer station first?"
2. **Check your bill and cart.** Cart lids and invoices sometimes list the service area or facility.
3. **Ask the city.** Contact Rosemount city hall (Public Works / Environmental) about hauling rules and whether the city requires haulers to report disposal sites.
4. **Dakota County Environmental Resources.** Visit dakotacountymn.gov, search "Recycling and Waste" and open the Reports page for the Solid Waste Progress Report and the Solid Waste Management Plan; email or phone the Environmental Resources department and ask where licensed haulers dispose of county waste. Haulers must be licensed with the county and file annual reports, so staff can tell you.
5. **MPCA data.**
   - "What's in My Neighborhood" (webapp.pca.state.mn.us/wimn): search "Pine Bend Landfill" or "Burnsville Sanitary Landfill" for permits and status. (This page loads dynamically; it did not return content to my tool, so use a browser.)
   - MPCA SCORE reports (annual county recycling data) and the MPCA Solid Waste Policy Report list facilities and tonnages.
   - MPCA's list of permitted solid waste facilities identifies transfer stations and MRFs.
6. **See it in person.** Pine Bend Landfill is a large engineered hill along the Mississippi bluffs in Inver Grove Heights, visible from Hwy 52/55 area roads; garbage trucks queue on nearby roads. Highland's trucks may be followed on a pickup day only as far as is safe and legal; calling is easier.
7. **Caveat.** Haulers can change facilities between years, and a single hauler may use several. Treat any answer as "as of today."


## Uncertainties and caveats
- The core question (Highland's disposal site) is unresolved from public sources. Confidence is low for the specific facility, high for the surrounding facts.
- Several sources are older newspaper articles (landfill ownership, capacity). Dakota County's 2024 report numbers are recent but do not name facilities.
- The county report's text says a "less than 1 percent" increase from 2023 while its table implies about 2%.
- Business-directory listings for Highland Sanitation are low quality; the Newport address is unverified.
