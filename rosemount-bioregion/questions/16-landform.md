# What primary ecological event/process influenced the land form where you live? (Bonus special: what's the evidence?)

## Short answer
The landscape under Rosemount is a product of the **last glaciation (Late Wisconsin, roughly 20,000 years ago)**. The Superior lobe, an ice tongue from the Lake Superior basin, pushed into the Twin Cities area and dropped the St. Croix moraine (hilly ground north of Rosemount). Meltwater pouring out of the ice then laid down a broad, nearly flat sheet of sand and gravel, the **Rosemount Outwash Plain**, which is the surface you stand on. Afterward, meltwater and floods incised the Mississippi River valley to the east and the Vermillion River valley to the south.

## The details

### The sequence of events
1. **Bedrock framework (hundreds of millions of years ago).** Under the glacial sediment are Ordovician-age sedimentary rocks. Under Rosemount, the first bedrock is typically the Prairie du Chien Group dolomite, roughly 50-100 feet below the surface on a Rosemount outwash site studied in 2013. The Dakota County Geologic Atlas (Minnesota Geological Survey, 2023) maps the county's bedrock plateaus, valleys, and a deep buried bedrock valley near Inver Grove Heights, with up to 520 feet of glacial sediment filling it at its deepest. That buried valley is an older Mississippi River channel.
2. **Superior lobe advance (about 20,000 years ago).** A marker at Hastings (Geological Society of Minnesota, 1998) describes the Superior lobe advancing out of the Lake Superior basin, filling the old Mississippi valley between St. Paul and Hastings with ice and sediment, then depositing the St. Croix moraine, a ridge of hills curving around the Twin Cities to the south and west.
3. **Outwash plain.** The same source says meltwater broke through the moraine and built a broad sand-and-gravel outwash plain that buried the Mississippi valley south of the moraine as far as Hastings. A Rosemount environmental review (2013) describes the local surface geology as outwash gravel and sand from Superior lobe meltwater (Cromwell Formation), late Wisconsin in age, with more than 35% gravel coarser than 2 mm. The MN DNR places Rosemount at the southern edge of its "St. Paul-Baldwin Plains and Moraines" subsection, and notes that the subsection's southern boundary coincides with the southern edge of the Rosemount Outwash Plain. Outwash plains there are described as level to rolling.
4. **Des Moines lobe (about 16,000 years ago).** The Hastings marker says the Des Moines lobe advanced from the northwest and its meltwater carved a river valley to the Mississippi at Hastings; the modern Vermillion River is a small remnant of that valley. (The river then runs about 60 miles and drains about 335 square miles.)
5. **Ice-block melt and river incision.** Buried ice blocks melted slowly and collapsed parts of the moraine, re-establishing the Mississippi valley as a drainage route; later meltwater flows formed river terraces and re-exposed the bedrock valley.
6. **Glacial River Warren (about 11,700 to 9,400 years ago).** Outflow from Glacial Lake Agassiz roared down the Minnesota River valley and merged with the Mississippi at the Twin Cities, carving a gorge and retreating waterfall (River Warren Falls, 175 ft high). This did not flow across Rosemount itself, but its floods deepened the Mississippi valley downstream, including the bluffs at Pine Bend a few miles to the east of Dodd Blvd.
7. **Holocene (11,700 years to now).** Prairie, oak savanna, and wetlands developed on the outwash; wind-blown silt (loess) capped some surfaces; the modern soils formed (see question 03).

### What the land form looks like
- **Rosemount Outwash Plain**: flat to gently rolling; elevation about 960-970 feet (Rosemount city is listed at 965 ft).
- **Moraine to the north** (St. Croix moraine): rolling to hummocky with steep short slopes and many lakes in kettles. Lebanon Hills in Eagan/Apple Valley is a nearby example.
- **Valley edges**: the Mississippi River bluffs to the east (Pine Bend) and the Vermillion River valley to the south.

### Evidence (the "bonus")
| Evidence | What it shows | Where to see it |
|---|---|---|
| **Flat topography** | Meltwater streams spread sediment evenly over a broad, braided plain | Look east or west along any long straight Rosemount road; very little change in elevation across miles |
| **Sand and gravel mines** | Thick, well-sorted, rounded sand and gravel is what meltwater leaves; the Dakota Aggregates mine at UMore Park covers 600 acres of an estimated 150-million-ton deposit | UMore Park (Rosemount), from public roads such as CSAH 46 / Bagley Ave (confirm access) |
| **Rounded pebbles of mixed rock types** | The Superior lobe carried reddish rocks (basalt, sandstone, gabbro) from the Lake Superior basin | Gravel pits, road cuts, and landscaping gravel from local pits. The till of the Superior lobe is red. |
| **Well logs** | Show sand/gravel over bedrock, with bedrock at roughly 50-100 ft at some Rosemount sites | MN County Well Index (see below) |
| **Deep groundwater** | Highly permeable outwash lets rain seep down; groundwater sat more than 40 ft down at the 2013 site and water levels in nearby wells about 108 ft down | Well logs, county atlas |
| **Soils** | Prairie Mollisols (Wadena, Waukegan, Estherville) loam over sand and gravel on outwash | Question 03 |
| **Kettle lakes and moraine hills to the north** | Ice-block melt-pits and ice-edge deposits | Lebanon Hills Regional Park, Eagan |
| **Terraces and bluffs along the Mississippi** | River incision after the ice | Pine Bend Bluffs SNA, Hastings |

## How to find and verify this yourself

1. **Geologic atlas (the authoritative source).** Search for "Geologic Atlas of Dakota County C-57" at the Minnesota Geological Survey (MGS) (mgs.umn.edu) or at the University of Minnesota Digital Conservancy. Part A (2023) has bedrock plates. Part B (Minnesota DNR) covers the sand and gravel, hydrogeology, and surficial material. Older Atlas C-6 (1990) has a Quaternary geology plate at 1:100,000. Zoom to Rosemount, check the map unit legend, then identify the unit under your home (central Rosemount is about 44.74 N, 93.12 W). The DNR's "Dakota County Geologic Atlas" web page has an interactive map in the DNR "Geologic Atlas Data" viewer.
2. **Minnesota Geospatial Commons** (gisdata.mn.gov): search "Dakota County surficial geology" or "Quaternary geology" and load the data in a free GIS tool, or in the online "MN Geologic Atlas Viewer."
3. **Well record.** Go to the **MN County Well Index** (MDH, health.state.mn.us, search "County Well Index" > "CWI Online" > map). Zoom to your location; click wells within a half mile. The "Stratigraphy" tab lists layers (e.g., "sand and gravel" or "clay") with depths, and the bedrock name and depth. Compare at least three wells.
4. **In the field.**
   - **Landform**: walk a half-mile in each direction and notice how little the ground rises and falls; then drive north toward Apple Valley or Eagan and notice the hilly moraine.
   - **Gravel pit**: from a legal vantage, look at the pit wall for stratified sand and gravel layers; check pebble types (red volcanic rocks and agates from the Lake Superior basin).
   - **Digging**: a utility trench or garden hole (call 811 first) will hit sand/gravel at 2-3 feet.
5. **LiDAR hillshade**: use the Minnesota Elevation Mapping tool (MnTOPO, "MnTOPO" from DNR) to see a shaded-relief map of the area; flat outwash plain, kettle holes, and river terraces stand out.
6. **Read the markers.** The Geological Society of Minnesota / MnDOT geology marker in Hastings (on Hwy 55) has the plain-language summary of the Superior lobe and Des Moines lobe story.

## Uncertainties and caveats
- I could not open the Dakota County Geologic Atlas surficial plates or the Quaternary map, so I have not confirmed the exact map unit under your location. The statement that the surface is Superior-lobe outwash rests on the DNR ECS description, the 2013 Rosemount EAW, and the Hastings marker. If your site lies on the thin Des Moines lobe or loess-covered terrace, the sediments may differ in detail.
- Some sources attribute the Rosemount Outwash Plain to the St. Croix phase, others to the later Des Moines lobe advance; ages of "about 20,000 years" (Superior lobe) and "about 16,000 years" (Des Moines lobe) come from the Hastings marker and are approximate.
- Whether Glacial River Warren flows touched the Rosemount area itself is not documented in the sources I read; I describe it only as a downstream influence on the Mississippi valley.
- The research used general Rosemount coordinates, not a specific lot (see question 03).
