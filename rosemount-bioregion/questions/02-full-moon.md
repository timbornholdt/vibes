# How many days till the moon is full? (Slack of two days allowed.)

## Short answer
As of **Friday, November 6, 2026**, the next full moon is **Tuesday, November 24, 2026 at 8:54 a.m. CST** (14:54 UTC). That is **18 calendar days** away, or about **17.9 days** counting from midday on Nov 6. It is the **Beaver Moon**. In Ojibwe it is **Gashkadino-giizis** (Freezing Over Moon). It is also the first of **three supermoons in a row** (Nov 2026, Dec 2026, Jan 2027).

Tonight's moon is a thin **waning crescent**, about 26 days into its cycle. **New moon** comes at **1:02 a.m. CST on Monday, Nov 9**. After that, the moon will grow each evening until Nov 24.

## The details

### Why the moon has phases, and the 29.5-day cycle
The moon is always half lit by the Sun. What we see depends on the angle between the Sun, Earth and Moon.
- At **new moon**, the moon sits roughly between Earth and the Sun, so its lit side faces away from us.
- At **full moon**, the moon is on the far side of Earth from the Sun. Astronomers define the exact moment as when the Moon's ecliptic longitude is 180° from the Sun's.

One full cycle, new moon to new moon, is the **synodic month**. Its *mean* length is **29.530588853 days**, or 29 d 12 h 44 m 3 s. That is longer than the moon's 27.32-day orbit relative to the stars (the *sidereal* month). The reason is that Earth moves about 1/12 of the way around the Sun in that time, so the moon must travel a little extra to line up with the Sun again.

Individual months are not all the same length. The moon's orbit is elliptical and the Sun tugs on it, so a given synodic month can be about 6–7 hours longer or shorter than average. Over 2026–2035, a full moon can land as much as **~10 hours early or ~18 hours late** compared with the "mean" prediction. That is why apps use a real ephemeris rather than just the average.

A full moon is a single **instant**, the same moment everywhere on Earth. Only the clock time shown changes with your time zone. To the eye, the moon looks "full" for about a day on either side.

### Dakota moon names (Mdewakaŋtuŋwaŋ Dakota)
The Shakopee Mdewakanton Sioux Community is the closest Dakota community to Rosemount, about 20 miles west. Its cultural site, Hoċokata Ṫi, lists the Dakota moons in order, starting in spring, since the Dakota New Year (Ómaka Téca) begins in spring. The Dakota count **13 moons** in some years, adding *Wanicokaya Wi* (Midwinter Moon) or *Bdokecokaya Wi* (Midsummer Moon).

SMSC does **not** tie these to Gregorian months. The "approx. month" column below is only an inference from the order, assuming the list starts around April. Treat it as a rough guide, not a fixed rule.

Indigenous moon names generally name a whole **lunar month**, the time from one new moon to the next. They are not just a name for the full-moon night. They also follow what is happening on the land: rice harvest, ice forming, sap running. So they can drift against the Gregorian calendar.

## How to find and verify this yourself
1. **U.S. Naval Observatory (official U.S. source).** Go to aa.usno.navy.mil, choose Data → "Phases of the Moon", and enter 2026. Times are given in UT. Subtract 6 hours for CST, or 5 hours for CDT (mid-March to early November). For Nov 24, 2026, look for a full moon near 14:53–14:54 UT.
2. **timeanddate.com.** Open Moon → Moon Phases and pick Minneapolis or Saint Paul. Rosemount is in the same time zone, and the instant of full moon does not depend on where you are. The page also shows illumination % and moonrise times.
3. **Old Farmer's Almanac**, "Full Moon dates for Rosemount, MN" (link in Sources). This lists every full moon of the year in Central Time.
4. **Count the days.** Subtract today's date from the full-moon date. If the full moon falls in the early morning, as at 8:54 a.m. on Nov 24, the moon will look nearly full on the evenings of both Nov 23 and Nov 24.
5. **Watch the sky.**
   - The waxing moon is in the **evening** sky and grows each night.
   - The **terminator** (the line between light and dark) gets straighter at first quarter, Nov 16–17.
   - Near full, the moon **rises in the east around sunset**, about 4:40 p.m. in late November in Rosemount, and sets around sunrise.
   - Moonrise comes roughly 50 minutes later each night.
   - Good open eastern views nearby include the higher ground of the Rosemount area and the open fields east toward UMore Park.
6. **Caveat on "supermoon".** This is a popular term, not an official astronomical one. Definitions vary; Space.com uses "within 90% of perigee". The size difference is hard to see without a side-by-side photo.


## Uncertainties and caveats
- **Exact minute.** The ephemeris here gives 14:54:04 UTC for Nov 24; Space.com and the Almanac show 14:53 or 8:55 local because of rounding. Trust USNO for the official minute.
- **Moon names vary.** The "traditional" English names come largely from the Old Farmer's Almanac and are loosely based on Algonquian and colonial names; they are not a single tribal tradition.
- **Ojibwe names** differ by community and dialect. Ojibwe.net lists several options for most months, and the NPS Apostle Islands page gives a 13-moon sequence without fixed months.
- **Dakota names** here come from SMSC. Other Dakota or Lakota communities use different names and timings, and SMSC gives no Gregorian month mapping. The month column above is my inference.
- **Lunar eclipses.** The Feb, Jul and Aug 2027 events are **penumbral only**. They cause faint dimming that is often not noticeable. Whether each is visible depends on whether the moon is above the horizon in Minnesota at the time.
