# When do the deer rut in your region, and when are the young born?

## Short answer
The white-tailed deer near Rosemount rut in November. Minnesota DNR research staff put peak breeding at about **November 12-14**, inside a short window that is triggered by shortening days rather than by weather. Pregnancy lasts about 195-200 days, so fawns are born mostly **mid-May to mid-June**, with the bulk in the last week of May and the first half of June. Today (Nov 6) you are in the pre-rut / early-rut run-up to the peak, which is also the worst stretch of the year for deer-vehicle collisions.

## The details

### The annual cycle (white-tailed deer, southern Minnesota)

| Phase | Typical timing | What is happening |
|---|---|---|
| Velvet shedding | Late Aug - Sept | Bucks' antlers harden and the velvet is rubbed off. |
| Early pre-rut | Mid-Oct to late Oct | Bucks start making rubs (bark scraped off saplings) and scrapes (pawed bare patches under an overhanging branch). Bucks' necks swell. |
| Pre-rut | Late Oct to ~Nov 7 | Bucks check scrapes and trail does; chasing begins but few does are receptive. |
| **Peak breeding** | **~Nov 10-20, centred on Nov 12-14** | Does come into estrus (about a day each); bucks tend, chase, and fight. Highest daytime buck movement. |
| Post-rut / second rut | Late Nov - early Dec | Does that were not bred cycle again about 28 days later, so a smaller second burst of activity can occur. Bucks are exhausted. |
| Gestation | ~195-200 days | Fawn growth is mostly in the last third (March-May). |
| **Fawning** | **Mid-May to mid-June; peak ~May 25 - June 10** | Does isolate from the herd, hide newborns in cover. |
| Weaning / fawn spots lost | Aug - Sept | Spots fade by about 3-4 months. |

The rut timing in the table's "pre-rut" and "second rut" rows is general whitetail biology that I did not verify in a Minnesota DNR page during this session; the November peak date, gestation length and fawning window below are source-backed.

### What the sources say
- A Star Tribune article quoting the DNR's wildlife research manager says Minnesota's peak breeding is around **Nov. 12-14**, in a narrow window set by declining daylight, and gestation is about **195 days**, meaning fawns are dropped in May. (Star Tribune "In the rut")
- A second Star Tribune piece says most fawns are born in **late May and early June**, quotes a gestation range of roughly 196-213 days, and notes first-time mothers usually have one fawn while older does often have twins and sometimes triplets. (Star Tribune "Fawns are arriving")
- A DNR bulletin says deer breed in **November or early December**, that females give birth after about seven months to one to three fawns, and that most fawns are born **mid-May to mid-June**. It stresses that a fawn lying alone is usually not abandoned. (DNR bulletin)

### Why the timing is so tight
Because deer breeding is triggered by shortening day length (photoperiod), the rut dates barely shift from year to year. Weather can shift the *visible* activity (a warm spell suppresses daytime movement; the first cold snap boosts it), but the biological peak is fixed. Fawning happens in late spring so that fawns have the whole green season to grow before their first winter. Back-calculating: a doe bred on Nov 13 with a 197-day gestation drops fawns around **May 29**.

### Signs you can see around Rosemount
- **Rubs:** vertical strips of bark scraped off trunks 1-4 inches thick, roughly knee-to-waist height, with exposed pale wood. Fresh ones are bright.
- **Scrapes:** bare dirt ovals about 2-3 feet across beneath an overhanging "licking branch." Expect them along field edges and woodlot trails at places such as the Vermillion Highlands (hunting is permitted there year-round in various forms, so wear blaze orange during firearm seasons).
- **Behaviour:** bucks trailing does with nose low and neck stretched; does running ahead; bucks crossing roads at all hours, not just dawn and dusk.
- **Tracks:** in snow, a buck's tracks may show hoof drag, but this is not a reliable sex indicator.

### Collision risk on Rosemount roads
MnDOT and law-enforcement messaging consistently describes **November as the peak month for deer-vehicle crashes in Minnesota**, because rutting deer move more and unpredictably and because dawn/dusk commutes fall in the dark after the end of daylight saving time. I did not find a crash count specific to Rosemount streets, so treat the risk as generally elevated on roads that border fields, wetlands or wooded corridors (Rosemount has these on its east and south sides). Practical tips: scan the ditches at dawn and dusk, slow down in "deer crossing" zones, if you see one deer expect another, and brake firmly in a straight line rather than swerve.

## How to find and verify this yourself
1. **MN DNR deer pages.** Go to dnr.state.mn.us and search "white-tailed deer" or "deer biology". The DNR's species profile and its deer-management pages describe breeding season and fawning. (I could not load the DNR's main deer page in this session, so confirm the numbers there.)
2. **iNaturalist phenology (fawns).** Go to inaturalist.org > Explore > set the species to *Odocoileus virginianus*, place to "Dakota County, Minnesota" (or draw a circle around Rosemount), then Filters > "Life Stage: Juvenile" or search the annotation "Fawn"/"Spotted", and open the **Seasonality** tab on the Species page. You should see a spike of fawn sightings from late May through June.
3. **iNaturalist (rut behaviour).** Same filter, but look for observations tagged "Mating behaviour"/"Rut" in the observation fields, or simply browse observations from Nov 5-20 for bucks with swollen necks chasing does.
4. **Trail camera.** Set a camera at a field edge or trail crossing from mid-October. Count the share of buck photos taken in daylight by week; you should see a rise through early November, a peak around Nov 10-20, then a drop. In late May, check the same camera for does with a single trailing fawn.
5. **Look in person now.** Walk the edge of a woodlot or wetland at the Vermillion Highlands, Whitetail Woods Regional Park or along the Vermillion River greenway in the early morning and look for fresh rubs and scrapes, then compare again in late November when they are old and dry.
6. **Fawn rule:** if you find a fawn alone in late May to June, leave it. The doe is nearby and returns to nurse; call a licensed wildlife rehabilitator (listed by the DNR) only if it is visibly injured or the doe is known to be dead.
7. **Collision data:** Minnesota Department of Public Safety (dps.mn.gov) Office of Traffic Safety publishes crash facts, and MnDOT's "Minnesota Crash Mapping Analysis Tool (MnCMAT)" can be filtered by county, month and "animal" crash type to see local patterns.

Caveats: the *peak* of breeding is a population-level statistic. Individual does breed anywhere from late October into December (and rare fawns are born as early as May or as late as August from late breeders).


## Uncertainties and caveats
- The Nov 12-14 peak is a statewide estimate from a DNR researcher's remarks as reported in a newspaper; farmland and metro deer can run a few days earlier or later.
- Gestation figures vary by source (195 to about 213 days) because of variation among does and in how breeding date is measured.
- Pre-rut/second-rut details come from general whitetail biology, not a verified Minnesota page.
- Crash risk specific to Rosemount streets was not found; the November peak is a statewide pattern.
- I did not verify the iNaturalist taxon ID.
