# What is the land use history of where you live?

## Short answer
For thousands of years the Rosemount outwash plain was prairie and oak savanna maintained by fire and used by Dakota people. After the 1851 Treaty of Mendota it was surveyed, claimed, and farmed. Dodd Blvd follows the line of the first major road through the area, built in 1853. The land stayed mostly agricultural until the late 20th century, when Rosemount's west side filled with subdivisions, mostly in the 1980s-90s. Your house's year built is a good marker for when your immediate neighborhood converted from field to housing. I have not confirmed the pre-subdivision use of any specific parcel.

## The details

### Timeline

| Period | What was happening | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Before ~1850 | Prairie and oak savanna on the outwash plain; Mdewakanton Dakota homeland | The MN DNR says tallgrass prairie occupied the level to gently rolling land in this part of the Twin Cities region, with oak and aspen savanna and bur oak savanna elsewhere, and fire as the most important disturbance. The UMN Rosemount Research and Outreach Center history says the land was used exclusively by the Mdewakanton band of Dakota before European arrival. |
| 1838 | First geographical surveys (Joseph Nicollet) | Per UMN. |
| Aug 5, 1851 | Treaty of Mendota | The Mdewakanton and Wahpekute Dakota ceded their lands to the U.S. in exchange for annuities (about $1.41 million for the land) and a reservation on the Minnesota River. Ratified June 23, 1852. |
| 1853 | Dodd Road built; William Strathern is first to claim land | Captain William B. Dodd's road ran from Mendota to St. Peter (the party left in April and took 109 days). The road runs through western Rosemount. |
| 1854 | Post office named "Rosemount" by first postmaster Andrew Keegan | Named for a town in Ireland. |
| 1858 | Rosemount Township organized | Chosen over "Saratoga" to reflect the Irish Catholic settlers. |
| 1860s-70s | Minnesota Central Railway; village platted (1866) and incorporated (1875) | A commercial center grew by the tracks just east of the Dodd Road. The community of Rich Valley (post office 1858-1935) was near today's 140th Street. |
| By 1870 | Farms cover about 80% of Dakota County's land | Per UMN. |
| May 1942 | Gopher Ordnance Works (GOW): farm families removed | The federal government took roughly 11,000 acres across Rosemount and Empire for a DuPont-operated smokeless powder plant; production ran only January-October 1945. |
| 1947-48 | University of Minnesota receives about 7,686 acres | Becomes UMore Park / Rosemount Research and Outreach Center. |
| 1955 | Pine Bend Refinery (Great Northern Oil, now Flint Hills Resources) | On the Rosemount/Inver Grove Heights border; the area's largest private employer. |
| 1970s | City forms | Sources conflict on exact dates (village and township merged 1971; city incorporation reported as 1972, 1974, or 1975). Population about 4,000 in 1970. |
| 1980s-90s | Suburban boom | Rosemount grew about 70% in each of the 1980s and 1990s. West-side subdivisions, mostly 1980s-90s, replaced farm fields. |
| 2013-present | Aggregate mining at UMore Park | Dakota Aggregates (Cemstone and Ames) mining about 600 acres of sand and gravel (estimated 150 million tons over about 40 years). |
| 2020 | Population 25,650 | A 435-acre residential development on part of the old GOW site has been announced. |

### The Dodd Road connection
Dodd Blvd is named for the road builder Captain William B. Dodd. A 2003 National Register listing covers three surviving segments of the Dodd Road in Le Sueur and Rice counties. Wikipedia notes that much of the original route is now paved and signed in West St. Paul, Mendota Heights, Rosemount, Apple Valley, and Lakeville. I did not find a source that traces the exact alignment block by block, so I cannot say whether today's Dodd Blvd sits exactly on the 1853 track, but the road in Rosemount did run through the west side where Dodd Blvd is today.

### What's likely under the pavement in a west-side subdivision
- **Pre-1850**: prairie/savanna on loamy-over-sand outwash soils (see question 03).
- **1850s-1980s**: most probably cropland or pasture owned by farm families (corn, small grains, hay are typical on these soils). I have no parcel-specific record.
- **1980s-90s**: platting of subdivision additions and street grid, topsoil stripped and replaced.
- **Today**: single-family housing, with commercial and apartment parcels along Dodd Blvd.

## How to find and verify this yourself

1. **Historical aerial photos (best for "what was here in the 1930s-1990s?")**
   - Go to the University of Minnesota's **Minnesota Historical Aerial Photographs Online (MHAPO)** at apps.lib.umn.edu/mhapo (also reachable from geo.lib.umn.edu/mhapo; the John R. Borchert Map Library hosts it).
   - Choose **Dakota County** and a year (coverage runs roughly 1920s to 2000, check which Dakota County years are listed; I did not verify them). Use the map to find your location, or type the address; click the photo footprint covering it and open the image. Compare the earliest, a mid-century, a 1970s-80s, and a 1990s image.
   - Free alternative: **USGS EarthExplorer** (earthexplorer.usgs.gov), data set "Aerial Photo Single Frames"; also the **Google Earth Pro "Historical Imagery" slider** (clock icon).
2. **Original land survey (what the surveyors recorded in the 1850s)**
   - Minnesota's **Original Public Land Survey plats and field notes** are online from MnGeo: mngeo.mn.gov/chouse/GLO/index.html (plats) and mngeo.mn.gov/chouse/GLO/fieldnotes/index.html (field notes). Find **Township 115 North, Range 19 West (5th PM)** for west Rosemount (R18W for the east side), then the section named in your parcel's legal description. The notes record "bearing trees" (species and diameter) and a one-line land description such as "level prairie, soil good".
3. **Plat books and old maps**: the Borchert Map Library holds county plat books (circa 1870s-1950s) that show owner names on each quarter section. Use the Library's online catalog (lib.umn.edu/borchert).
4. **Subdivision records**: use the **Dakota County Property Search / parcel viewer** (co.dakota.mn.us, "Property Records," or GIS parcel map). Look up your parcel for the plat name (for example "Something 3rd Addition"), year built, and legal description. The County Recorder's office has the recorded plat.
5. **Local history**: ask the **Rosemount Area Historical Society** (and the Dakota County Historical Society in South St. Paul) for farm family histories, old farmstead photos, and the Dodd Road alignment. The Rosemount Research and Outreach Center history page and the Digital Conservancy at UMN hold reports on UMore Park.
6. **Environmental history**: the University's Phase I Environmental Site Assessment for UMore Park (in the UMN Digital Conservancy) summarizes land use on the GOW site.
7. **In person**: look for old windbreaks (rows of cottonwood, box elder, or spruce), farmstead remnants such as lilacs or old trees at odd places, and the diagonal course of Dodd Blvd, which departs from the section-line grid.

## Uncertainties and caveats
- The parcel-level record (farm owner names, what was grown) is not in any source I could reach; use MHAPO and the plat books to fill it in.
- The research used general Rosemount coordinates, not a specific parcel (see question 03).
- Sources conflict on Rosemount's incorporation date (1971-1975).
- GOW acreage is cited as 11,000 acres by one source; the University history says 7,686 acres were deeded to it in 1947-48; these are different measures (taken vs. transferred).
- The Wikipedia pages used here are secondary sources; cross-check dates with the Rosemount Area Historical Society.
- Whether the specific Dodd Blvd alignment follows the 1853 Dodd Road track is not confirmed.
