# What are the major plant associations in your region?

## Short answer
You are in the **Eastern Broadleaf Forest Province**, in the **St. Paul-Baldwin Plains and Moraines Subsection**. Per the DNR, the original landscape was a mosaic dominated by **oak and aspen savanna**, with common **tallgrass prairie** (concentrated on level to gently rolling land such as outwash plains) and **maple-basswood forest** limited to places protected from fire, like steep ravines. Wetland communities (marshes, wet prairie, seepage swamps and floodplain forest) occupy stream and river corridors. Today urban development is the main land use.

## The details

### Where Rosemount sits in the classification
- **Province:** Eastern Broadleaf Forest.
- **Subsection:** St. Paul-Baldwin Plains and Moraines. The DNR says this subsection's southern boundary coincides with the southern edge of the Rosemount Outwash Plain. So Rosemount is on or near the southern edge of the subsection, and the classification is solid but one should not assume the edge is crisp on the ground. (I could not read the exact DNR section name from the page text, so I do not state it here; the ECS map lookup below will show it.)
- Landforms: St. Croix Moraine (Superior lobe) to the north, level to rolling outwash plains to the south, with drift mostly under 100 ft thick.
- Climate: about 28 in (north) to 31 in (south) of precipitation; growing season about 146-156 days (DNR subsection page).

### Presettlement associations (pre-1850s)
The DNR describes the mosaic as dominated by oak and aspen savanna, with:

| Association | Where it was | Characteristic species |
|---|---|---|
| Tallgrass prairie | Level to gently rolling land, i.e. outwash plains | Big bluestem, little bluestem, Indiangrass, switchgrass, prairie smoke, pasqueflower, many forbs |
| Bur oak / oak savanna (or "oak openings") | Moraine ridges, ravines, dry sandy-gravelly outwash | Bur oak, northern pin oak, grass and forb understory; see "Dry Sand-Gravel Oak Savanna (Southern)" in DNR Native Plant Community classification |
| Aspen-oak savanna and woodland | Parts of the subsection, transitional | Quaking aspen, oaks, hazel brush |
| Maple-basswood forest | Only where fire was blocked: steep ravines, certain stream orientations | Sugar maple, basswood, red oak, bitternut hickory |
| Wetlands and floodplain | Vermillion River, Mississippi corridor, seepage areas | Wet prairie and marsh sedges, black ash seepage swamp (seen at Pine Bend Bluffs SNA), floodplain forest |

Fire (set by lightning and by Dakota and other Indigenous people) kept the savanna and prairie open; forests persisted only where topography shielded them.

### Marschner map
Francis Marschner's map of "original vegetation of Minnesota" (compiled from the 1847-1907 General Land Office survey records) is the standard statewide presettlement vegetation layer, available as a GIS dataset from the Minnesota Geospatial Commons. I was not able to open the data and read the polygon value at a specific point, so I cannot tell you which Marschner class (prairie vs. oak openings and barrens vs. forest) covers your home. Based on the DNR description of the Rosemount Outwash Plain I expect prairie and/or oak openings, but this is an inference, not a verified reading. Treat the answer as "prairie or oak savanna" until you check the map yourself (steps below).

### Native plant communities you can still find in Dakota County
- **Dry Sand-Gravel Prairie / Oak Savanna (Southern):** the best match to Rosemount outwash soils. Seen at Hastings Sand Coulee SNA and in dry prairie at Pine Bend Bluffs SNA (dry prairie, oak forest, white pine on north-facing slopes, black ash seepage swamp; over 150 plant species including wild geranium, hoary puccoon, and the state-threatened kittentails).
- **Dry Bedrock Bluff Prairie, Mesic Prairie, Pin Oak-Bur Oak Woodland, Red Oak-Sugar Maple-Basswood forest, Red Oak-White Oak forest:** listed for the Vermillion Prairie and Oak Woods area in southern Dakota County (DNR), where rocky outliers sheltered woods from prairie fires.
- **Southern Dry-Mesic Oak Woodland, Southern Floodplain Forest, Southern Mesic Maple-Basswood Forest, Central Mesic Hardwood Forest** are named by the DNR as spotlight communities across the Twin Cities metro subsections.

### Current land cover
The DNR describes urban development as the primary land use in the subsection, with only small forested areas remaining. Near Rosemount specifically, expect: turf and landscaping (Kentucky bluegrass, ornamental trees); cropland to the south and east; mined and reclaimed ground; the UMore/Vermillion Highlands area with restored and old-field grassland; and wetlands and ponds in the drainage network. I did not find a quantified land-cover breakdown for Rosemount in this session; the National Land Cover Database (below) will give you one.

## How to find and verify this yourself

1. **DNR Ecological Classification System.** Go to dnr.state.mn.us/ecs. Use the interactive/PDF maps of provinces, sections and subsections, find the Twin Cities, and confirm that Rosemount falls in "St. Paul-Baldwin Plains and Moraines" (code 222Md). The subsection page has the landscape description cited here.
2. **Marschner map in GIS.** Search "Native Vegetation at the Time of the Public Land Survey" (Marschner) on gisdata.mn.gov / the Minnesota Geospatial Commons. Download the shapefile or open the web map, type your address into the address search, and click the polygon to read its class. A scanned/printed version is also available through the Borchert Map Library at the University of Minnesota.
3. **Original surveyor notes.** The 1850s GLO survey notes for your township record "bearing trees" and describe each mile line as prairie, oak openings, or timber. The Minnesota Historical Society and Minnesota Geospatial Commons host the plats; find your township and section by PLSS (use the Minnesota Land Ownership/PLSS viewer or Dakota County's GIS map).
4. **Minnesota Biological Survey (MBS).** On the DNR site, use the MBS "Sites of Biodiversity Significance" and native plant community layers (also on the Geospatial Commons and the DNR's interactive "MBS Map Viewer") to see what high-quality remnants exist near Rosemount.
5. **Walk a remnant.** Pine Bend Bluffs SNA (North Unit trailhead near N44 47.270, W93 1.786 per the listing; check the DNR SNA page for current directions), Hastings Sand Coulee SNA and the Vermillion River corridor show what the associations look like. Look for oak-grassland edges, bluff prairie with little bluestem, and shady slopes with sugar maple and basswood.
6. **Check what is growing around you.** On iNaturalist, Explore > Plants > Dakota County, then sort by observations; native community indicators include bur oak, big and little bluestem, pasqueflower and prairie smoke. Note which are planted vs. wild.
7. **Current land cover.** Use the USGS National Land Cover Database (mrlc.gov viewer) and click on your location for a modern classification (developed, cultivated, grassland, etc.).

Caveats: Marschner is a 1930s map based on 1850s surveys at a coarse scale (it generalizes small features), and boundaries between prairie, savanna and forest were gradational.

## Uncertainties and caveats
- Exact Marschner class at your home: unverified (see above).
- The DNR subsection page does not use the terms "oak openings" or "Big Woods"; the Big Woods is a separate subsection to the west and southwest, not at Rosemount.
- The DNR's native plant community classification is being updated, so community names may change.
- Land cover percentages were not obtained.
